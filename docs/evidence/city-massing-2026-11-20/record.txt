City massing — before and after
===============================

Captured 2026-11-20 against https://dhaka.nonarkara.org, in headless Chrome
(scripts/lib/cdp.mjs), at the same camera for each pair: Dhaka city centre,
90.4125 E / 23.8103 N, bearing 20°, pitch 55°.

The change
----------
The city lens drew every building as a footprint: a fill with no height. The
lens was called "City in 3D" and the 3D/2D control changed almost nothing a
reader could see. The streamed tiles do carry a height — the provider's
computed render_height — and the atlas was standing on the truth of its own
excuse while drawing nothing. The description under the lens read "streamed
tiles do not expose source height tags". They do.

The city lens now draws that massing, the way the street lens always did, and
says where it comes from. The height is the map provider's computed height,
not a survey; a building with no height in the source stays flat.

Measured, same camera, before and after
---------------------------------------
Before (build 0cc7857):
  rendered features in city-buildings     93
  features carrying a provider height      92
  median provider height                   15 m
  lens description  "Zoom in for building footprints; select a feature for
                     source details. OSM footprints; streamed tiles do not
                     expose source height tags."

After (build f061144):
  rendered features in city-buildings     93
  features carrying a provider height      92
  median provider height                   15 m
  lens description  "Zoom in for the building massing and footprints; select
                     a feature for source details. 3D: the massing is the map
                     provider's computed height, not a survey; a building with
                     no height in the source stays flat."

The feature count and the heights are identical — the data did not change.
What changed is that the atlas stands on it, and says what it stands on.

At z14.6 the difference is small: the buildings are a few pixels tall in a
metropolitan view, and the satellite imagery still reads. At z16.3 the massing
is what a reader actually sees — the dense fabric of Old Dhaka reads as a
city with walls and a sun on them, the industrial sheds of Mirpur read as low
blocks, and the imagery shows between them. That is the scale the change is
for.

What did not change
-------------------
The metropolitan view (the lens's default camera, z12.5) is untouched: the
tiles carry no buildings below z14, so the overview of the river ring, the
metro lines and the industrial belts is exactly as it was. The 2D view still
shows flat footprints. The street lens draws its own massing and is
unchanged.

Provenance
----------
Heights: OpenStreetMap via OpenFreeMap / OpenMapTiles, `render_height` and
`render_min_height`, clamped to 1–500 m; a building with no height in the
source stays flat.
Footprints: OpenStreetMap ODbL · OpenFreeMap.
Solar light: NOAA solar position for Dhaka, map-anchored.
Colour: the atlas's own Palette 1 by mapped use.

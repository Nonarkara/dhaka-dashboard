Architecture of a Civic Satellite Atlas

How a satellite pixel becomes a measured reading on screen, how that reading is collected and delivered, and the contracts that keep every number honest. Written for the city that has to act on it.

00Abstract

Dhaka has 23 million people, a population grid nobody publishes for the city, and a government that decides where a road goes, where a pump goes and where a school goes. What it did not have, until this system, was a way to see any of it. This paper describes an instrument that reads the city from free satellite observation and free official statistics, measures the result in physical units, and refuses to print a number it cannot source.

01The problem this instrument answers

A city of this density is measured constantly by satellites that pass over it several times a day. The observation is free, continuous and global. What has been missing is not the imagery — it is the translation. A radiance raster is not a district; a brightness value is not an economy. The work is in the step between the two, and that step is where instruments usually go wrong, silently.

The three failures this design refuses:
  • A number with no upstream — typed in as if it were data.
  • A zero where a measurement is missing — indistinguishable from a real zero.
  • A plausible number in the wrong unit — the hardest to catch, because it looks right.

02System architecture

The system is five planes. Each one has one job, and each one can fail without taking the others down.

  • Observation plane — the free world
    Seven satellite instruments from NASA GIBS (1,330 published layers, no key), NASA POWER for the solar and climate reanalysis, World Bank indicators, Open-Meteo for weather and air, and the Bangladesh Bureau of Water Development Board for river stages. Nothing here is free of caveats; each carries its own licence, cadence and known limits, all recorded in the register below.
  • Measurement plane — pixels into physical units
    The only place in the system where a raster becomes a number. A satellite tile carries a palette index per pixel, not a quantity; the quantity lives in a separate colour map that the WMTS capabilities document names for each layer. This plane walks that whole chain and refuses to produce a figure when any link is broken.
  • Collection plane — a clock, not a visitor
    A scheduled collector runs every fifteen minutes, measures the city, archives every complete upstream response to object storage, and publishes a validated snapshot to the database. A visitor never starts an acquisition; a cold request reads what the clock already left. A snapshot is only published if all fourteen places carry all seven instruments and at least half of the measurements succeeded.
  • Edge plane — one envelope for every answer
    Twenty-seven routes on a single Worker, each answering JSON with the same shape: a status, a machine-readable reason, a timestamp, and a provenance tier. Rate limiting per client address, an expiring cache, a persistent snapshot store and an object-storage archive of raw upstream responses. Nothing escapes as an unhandled error page.
  • Presentation plane — what a resident is asked to act on
    Seventeen map lenses over one canvas; a thin instrument band carrying the country's clock, the live exchange rate and the national readings; and a collage panel that puts seven domains and seven instruments side by side for one district at a time. The geometry is flat by rule — no rounded corners, no shadows, no gradients — and the language is Bengali first.

03How a number is born

This is the core of the system, and it is worth stating in full because every number in the atlas is born this way. 

capabilities  →  colour map  →  palette index  →  physical value
   does it         what unit       what is this        what is the
   exist?          is this index?  pixel worth?        answer?
  1. The capabilities document is the authority. It names every layer, its tile matrix sets, its formats, its time dimension and its colour map. A layer id that is not in that document does not exist as far as this system is concerned, and a date the document does not list carries no observation.
  2. The colour map is fetched from the link the capabilities document gives, not from a filename we guessed. It maps each palette index to a range in the layer's own unit — kelvin for a temperature, nW/(cm² sr) for radiance, a fraction for an index. It also marks which indices mean “no observation”, which is how an absent pixel is kept out of an average.
  3. The tile is fetched, the PNG is decoded, and the palette index of the pixel over the place is read. The index is then looked up in the colour map to give a range; the midpoint of that range is the honest estimate of what the quantised bin stands for.
Three traps this chain has to survive, each of which has already been observed in the wild:
  • The blank placeholder. A provider publishes a date directory before the observation behind it is ingested, and serves that date as a flat, opaque, single-colour tile. It is HTTP 200, non-empty, and indistinguishable from data to a status check or a byte count. Only decoding it, and then asking how much of the window actually carries an observation, tells the truth.
  • The open-ended bin. A colour map's top bin is often written as an unbounded range. Its midpoint is a colour-map artefact, not a measurement — for the radiance map that midpoint is five hundred thousand, where the real reading is tens. Such a bin is reported as the lower bound it guarantees, and flagged.
  • The wrong unit. A layer measured with another layer's colour map yields the right shape in the wrong unit — the most dangerous failure, because it looks right. The colour map's declared unit is compared against the unit the atlas claims, and a mismatch stops the release.

Two more rules follow from the same chain. The observation date is resolved by measurement, not by guessing: the system walks back from the published date until a window carries enough real observation to read. And a reading is never a whole district — it is a sample over a stated window at a stated resolution, and it says so.

04The collage: seven domains, seven instruments

One reading per district is a reading. Seven domains, each answered by a different instrument and all measured over the same fourteen places, is a collage — and it is the collage that says something, because the interesting fact about a city is rarely in one measurement. It is in the disagreement.

One instrument per domain, so no two domains print the same measurement as their own finding. The national companions are dated World Bank series, so the two halves of the picture can be read against each other: what the instruments measured in one district, and what the state is doing about it nationally.

05Honesty contracts

The refusal contract: If an upstream is unreachable, times out, or returns malformed fields, the system answers “unavailable” with an explicit machine-readable reason code and a timestamp. It never invents a zero, never presents an empty widget as normal, and never converts a lack of measurement into an all-clear.

Every reading carries a provenance tier, so a reader can tell at a glance what kind of claim is being made.

Tier Meaning
measuredObserved by an instrument, at a stated time, over a stated window.
modeledComputed by a physical or statistical model, not observed.
referenceAn official register or gazetteer — a place exists; its numbers do not.
unavailableNot measured, with a reason.

06Collection and delivery

A visitor-triggered measurement is a measurement that depends on who is looking, when, and whether the upstream was kind enough that minute. So the system does not measure for visitors. A scheduled collector runs every fifteen minutes; it measures the whole city once, archives every complete upstream response to object storage so that any published number can be traced back to the bytes it came from, and writes a validated snapshot to the database.

  • A cold request reads the published snapshot. It never starts an acquisition.
  • A snapshot is published only if it is complete: fourteen places, seven instruments each, distinct instruments per place, and at least half the measurements succeeded.
  • A snapshot younger than four hours is fresh. Beyond that it is served as stale, with a reason code saying the collector is overdue. Beyond seven days it is not served at all.

07What happens when the world misbehaves

Degradation is designed, not improvised. Each failure has a named, machine-readable reason code, and each one is visible in the interface rather than hidden behind an empty widget.

SituationWhat the system does
A satellite publishes a date with no observation behind itWalks back to the newest date whose window actually carries observation, and states which date it used.
Cloud over one districtThat district's reading for that instrument is unavailable, with a reason. The other six instruments still report.
An upstream is downThe route answers unavailable with a reason code. The interface says which feed is missing instead of showing a stale number as if it were current.
The collector itself failsNo snapshot is published. The last valid one is served, and as it ages the response says stale, then refuses.

09How to check our claims

A claim in a paper is worth exactly what the reader can check. Every assertion this system makes about its own behaviour is enforced by a test that can be made to fail.

  • Fifteen test suites run on every release. One of them validates every satellite layer id, tile level and unit against the vendored 1,330-layer capabilities catalogue, checks the tile arithmetic against the published scale denominators, and checks that the tile cache cannot hold a failure for long.
  • A mutation harness breaks the code that each assertion guards — one defect at a time — and requires the suite to fail every time. A green test that cannot go red is a comment, and this project does not ship comments.
  • The release itself verifies its own bytes: the deployed assets are compared byte-for-byte against what was committed, and live satellite tiles are fetched over Dhaka and checked for real content before the release is declared live.
  • Every upstream response behind a published number is archived, so a reader can trace a figure back to the bytes it came from rather than trusting this paper.

10What this system cannot do

The limits are stated here because a civic instrument that overstates itself is worse than none.

  • It cannot count people directly. Population comes from a published model grid, and the atlas says so wherever it appears.
  • It cannot survey a building. Street-level massing is drawn from the map provider's computed height, and the interface counts how many visible buildings rest on one rather than on a survey.
  • It cannot read what a building is used for. Night radiance says a place is lit, not what the light is for.
  • A district reading is a sample over a stated window at a stated resolution, not a whole-district average. Ward-level polygons are not yet in the atlas, and until they are, the reading says what it actually covers.
  • The official flood warning service requires a registered credential the atlas does not hold, so the flood tier it shows is a modelled rainfall proxy, labelled as such, and never presented as an official warning.
